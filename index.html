The Survivorship HUB

One navigator-coordinated pathway from diagnosis through the balance of life, across acute and extended/permanent survivorship. Select a step to see what is screened, what is decided, who is needed and what passes to the next step.

Re-entry is built in. Recurrence or a new primary sends the person back to Steps 1–2 with acute re-screening. Changed needs or risk at any time send them back to Step 5 reassessment.

Every domain is active at every step. Each row is a domain owned by a thematic working group; each cell shows where the HUB currently places that content, including proposals the TWG has not yet reviewed. Select a step heading to open its detail.

Comorbidities and medical follow-up (TWG 1) has work at every step: its baselines are taken at intake and at the MDT, before any treatment is given, and it leads the treatment summary and risk stratification at the transition point.

Survivorship care is most at risk at handovers. The HUB keeps one coordinator, one risk profile and one care plan across the whole journey.

What one continuous HUB keeps at the end of treatment

Different teams lead at different steps: the treating team in Steps 2–3, then the Survivorship Board and, for high-risk survivors, a specialist long-term follow-up team from Step 4. The HUB keeps them in one pathway, with one file, one navigator and one risk profile.

  • The baselines taken, or proposed, at intake and at the MDT: cardiac function, bone density, fertility, nutrition, physical function, distress, cognition.
  • The treatment exposure record (agents, cumulative doses, RT fields, complications) that late-effect risk and the care plan depend on.
  • The named navigator, at the moment patients describe losing their safety net.
  • A single risk and needs profile, updated rather than rebuilt.
  • Supportive care already under way: prehabilitation, rehabilitation, psycho-oncology, return-to-work planning.

How the TWGs plug in

The HUB is the shared pathway of Task 8.2.7, documented in IF-SurvHub. Each thematic working group owns the content of its domain and reviews how it is placed in the HUB: at which step, by whom and with which output.

The two survivorship phases map onto the HUB: acute survivorship, from primary diagnosis through active treatment, covers Steps 1–3; the end-of-treatment assessment at Step 4 closes it and opens extended and permanent survivorship, Steps 5–6, where JANE-2 risk-stratified follow-up is implemented. TWG 1 risk groups set follow-up at Step 4, and the Hamburg “Life After Cancer” model (navigator, core team, Survivorship Board, care plan and handbook) follows the same logic.

TWG 4 (Nutrition, Physical Activity and Health Behaviours) spans treatment and prevention: malnutrition, cachexia, prehabilitation and rehabilitation are part of cancer care, while healthy eating, regular activity and stopping nicotine and alcohol support long-term health and lower the risk of comorbidities.

Overlap between TWG activities and HUB steps is expected: activities are the building blocks the HUB coordinates.
